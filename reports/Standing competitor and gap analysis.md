# Standing owns the before; now close the loop

Standing has no direct competitor in what it actually does: every PayPal-focused seller tool found on 7 Nov 2026 sells tracking sync, a single after-the-fact fix pitched as the cure for holds, and none watches PayPal's published hold reasons, checks products against PayPal's Acceptable Use Policy, or builds a cited case before PayPal asks. The closest analogue, Payout Guard, sells a health score, threshold forecast and prohibited-product scan at $29 a month, but only for Shopify Payments, which validates the category while leaving PayPal open. Standing's weaknesses are reach (no Shopify or WooCommerce app, no Stripe, sandbox only) and, more importantly for the next three days, that its story stops one step short: the pack is never delivered to PayPal, held money is never shown, and nothing helps the seller after a hold lands, which is where the loudest 2026 complaints sit. The highest score-per-day moves are an AG Grid Community upgrade that turns the orders table into a real "PayPal says / Standing says" ledger (sponsor prize plus Design), two cheap read-only PayPal fields (Seller Protection eligibility and withheld balance with hold T-codes), and a time-boxed attempt to send the cited pack as dispute evidence through the existing preview, cancel and send pipeline, ending in a clearly labelled sandbox settlement. Several tempting ideas from the market (win probabilities, a single banded health score, automatic refunds, "releases funds" copy) would break Standing's hard rules and should stay out.

## Eight tracking-sync apps sell one fix; nobody warns before the hold

The direct market is crowded and commoditised. Synctrack leads with **5.0 stars from 450 reviews** and tiers from free (10 orders) to $59.99 a month, adding a multi-gateway dispute dashboard, auto-filled dispute evidence and an auto-compiled store-policy PDF on its Pro plan ([Synctrack](https://apps.shopify.com/synctrack)). TrackiPal charges $4.99–$9.99 ([TrackiPal](https://apps.shopify.com/trackipal)), and PalSync's pricing card is byte-identical to TrackiPal's, which makes it a sibling rather than an independent rival ([PalSync](https://apps.shopify.com/paypal-palsync)). Proveway (4.9 from 160, courier mapping across 900+ carriers) ([Proveway](https://apps.shopify.com/add-paypal-tracking-by-uptrack)), PaySync ([PaySync](https://apps.shopify.com/paysync)), Paltrack ([Paltrack](https://apps.shopify.com/paltrack)) and Trackiy ([Trackiy](https://apps.shopify.com/trackiy)) fill out the Shopify shelf, while WooCommerce has Synctrack's plugin, last updated January 2024 ([WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/omega-add-paypal-tracking-for-woocommerce/)), and zorem's Advanced Shipment Tracking ([zorem](https://www.zorem.com/how-to-reduce-paypal-disputes-with-ast-pros-automatic-tracking-sync/)). AfterShip's PayPal tracking is an alpha, US-only, buyer-facing "Track with PayPal" feature, not a hold tool ([AfterShip](https://support.aftership.com/en/tracking/articles/15441781-paypal-package-tracking)).

Adjacent dispute products all start after the buyer files. Chargeflow takes **25% of recovered chargebacks** and handles PayPal inquiries end to end, but its alerts are card-network only ([Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/pricing)). Disputifier's best PayPal feature refunds at the inquiry stage, either immediately or one to two days before escalation, and it explicitly does not predict disputes ([Disputifier help](https://intercom.help/disputifier-bcd86c5ce710/en/articles/11043298-how-paypal-chargebacks-hurt-your-business-how-to-disputifier-s-paypal-alerts-help-you)). Stripe Smart Disputes auto-submits evidence "just before the dispute times out" and is locked to Stripe ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/smart-disputes)). Merchant-risk AI from LegitScript, G2 Risk Solutions, Sardine and Persona is sold to acquirers and platforms to find risky sellers, not to sellers themselves ([G2RS](https://g2risksolutions.com/mcc-verify/), [Sardine](https://sardine.ai/kyb)). The nearest thing to Standing is **Payout Guard**, launched 28 May 2026 with zero reviews: a 0–100 health score, a chargeback time-to-threshold forecast and a prohibited-product scan, all for Shopify Payments, with PayPal never mentioned ([Payout Guard](https://apps.shopify.com/payout-guard)). It proves sellers are being sold this idea in 2026, and it is the most likely fast follower onto PayPal.

The competitive copy is also where Standing can win on honesty. Almost every sync app says or implies that tracking releases funds, cuts reserves or prevents holds: Paltrack's title promises to stop PayPal payment holds and lists Seller Protection qualification as a plan feature ([Paltrack](https://apps.shopify.com/paltrack)), TrackiPal claims to cut holds and calls itself an "Official PayPal Approved Partner" without proof ([TrackiPal](https://apps.shopify.com/trackipal)), and Angell EYE speaks of getting holds released quickly ([Angell EYE](https://www.angelleye.com/product/paypal-shipment-tracking-numbers-woocommerce/)). Only zorem admits sync "does not guarantee wins" and names dispute types it cannot help with ([zorem](https://www.zorem.com/how-to-reduce-paypal-disputes-with-ast-pros-automatic-tracking-sync/)). No PayPal source guarantees release on tracking upload; a PayPal Community employee says only that tracking "might affect" the release date ([PayPal Community](https://www.paypal-community.com/t5/Transactions/Paypal-is-holding-funds-for-an-indeterminate-amount-of-time/td-p/3042614)). The low-star reviews map neatly onto features Standing already has: sync silently stopping and causing limits, back-sync spamming customers with PayPal shipping emails ([PalSync](https://apps.shopify.com/paypal-palsync)), and "PayPal isn't releasing funds" after installing a sync app ([TrackiPal reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/trackipal/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2&ratings%5B%5D=3)). Standing's read-back of what reached PayPal, its preview-and-cancel window and its explicit "tracking addresses only some hold reasons" are the answers, and they belong in the video.

## The loudest 2026 pain begins after the hold, where Standing is silent

Standing is prevention-only, but public complaints in 2026 concentrate on what happens once PayPal has acted. An analysis of 687 negative US Google Play reviews from July–August 2026 found **71 described a limited or closed account; 37 called it permanent, 23 followed document submission, 21 involved a new or dormant account, and 13 said no rule was named** ([Unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/paypal-account-limited-after-id-upload-reviews-2026)); the publisher notes this is not a prevalence measure. In April 2026 the UK Financial Ombudsman upheld a £4,000 reserve plus a 10% 60-day rolling reserve on a seller with a reported **1.01%** dispute rate and found PayPal has "no specific obligation" to explain its risk decision ([FOS DRN-5996562](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5996562.pdf)). That ruling matters twice for Standing: staying near PayPal's 1% guidance does not guarantee money keeps moving, and the pack must be framed as the seller's organised evidence, never as an argument about PayPal's reasoning.

Recovery stalls on document fit. Vendor guides describe ID matching the account name, proof of address dated within 12 months, carrier tracking or access logs, supplier invoices and a business explanation, submitted only through the Resolution Center ([Chargeflow guide](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/remove-paypal-account-limitations-for-merchants)), and one claims an incomplete package restarts the review clock ([SeamlessChex](https://www.seamlesschex.com/deep-dives/how-to-reactivate-a-frozen-paypal-merchant-service-after-a-2026-compliance-review)); both sell related services, and the official deadline (two weeks versus 30 days) is unresolved. New markets add their own failure points. Within weeks of the January 2026 Paga launch, Nigerian users reported holds on first freelance receipts, a restriction after a $1 test payment and bans after verification uploads, while Paga's CEO said "there is no widespread issue" ([WeeTracker Nigeria](https://weetracker.com/2026/02/24/paypal-nigeria-return-user-complaints-paga/)). Paga requires the PayPal name to match the Paga name and allows one PayPal per Paga account ([Paga blog](https://paga.blog/what-you-need-to-know-about-using-paypal-on-paga/)). Kenyan freelancers were asked for contracts, invoices and address proof many could not supply, with balances held up to 180 days ([WeeTracker Kenya](https://weetracker.com/2026/06/03/kenyans-furious-paypal-frozen-accounts-freelancers/)).

| Pain (2026 sources; ranking is judgement, not measurement) | Severity | Standing today |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent limitation, balance held up to 180 days | Highest | Not addressed |
| Limitation after ID or document submission; verification failing | High | Not addressed |
| New account limited on first receipts | High in new markets | Partly (new-seller status, launch plan) |

| Name-match and KYC friction on Paga withdrawals | Medium | Not addressed |

Two gaps are closable in plain code without predicting anything. A **"money held" ledger** can be built from PayPal's own data: the Balances API returns `withheld_balance` against `available_balance` with a refresh time, Transaction Search carries paired hold and release codes such as T2103/T2104 (reserve) and T2105/T2106 (payment review), and capture objects expose `status_details.reason` values like `PENDING_REVIEW` ([Transaction Search schema](https://developer.paypal.com/api/transaction-search/v1/schema.json), [event codes](https://developer.paypal.com/docs/transaction-search/transaction-event-codes/), [Payments schema](https://developer.paypal.com/api/payments/v2/schema.json)). A **hold-day response kit** can turn the vendor consensus into a checklist: confirm the notice inside PayPal rather than from an email link, record the case ID, requested documents and seller-entered deadline, check names and dates match, submit once and complete, and never open a second account. What Standing must not do is claim to read "account limitation status": the `CUSTOMER.ACCOUNT-LIMITATION.*` webhooks exist but sit in partner-managed, limited-release flows ([webhook names](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/webhooks/event-names/)). Service sellers' evidence (contract, invoice, delivery timestamp, acknowledgement) and a Nigeria identity-consistency checklist are real gaps too, but they demo less vividly and cost more per point.

## PayPal's dispute and balance APIs make the pack land somewhere real

The single most important platform finding is that the Disputes API lets a seller app **send evidence**, and sandbox has two test-only calls that make a full lifecycle demonstrable. `provide-evidence` accepts proof of fulfilment, refund, receipt, item description and more, mapping almost one-to-one onto Standing's evidence kinds; `require-evidence` moves a dispute into `WAITING_FOR_SELLER_RESPONSE`; and `adjudicate`, marked "for sandbox use only", settles the case the way a PayPal agent would ([Disputes v1 docs](https://developer.paypal.com/docs/api/customer-disputes/v1/), [schema](https://developer.paypal.com/api/customer-disputes/v1/schema.json)). The dispute object also returns `seller_response_due_date`, which gives a real countdown. Today Standing's headline, "builds the seller's case before PayPal asks", ends in a PDF; with this it ends in a sandbox write carrying a PayPal debug ID. Three unknowns make it risky: the multipart request shape was not read, whether a sandbox dispute reaches `UNDER_REVIEW` without manual steps is unconfirmed, and the app may need Disputes enabled with the `update-seller` scope.

Cheaper read-only fields lift Potential Impact for almost no effort. Every capture carries `seller_protection.status` (ELIGIBLE, PARTIALLY_ELIGIBLE, NOT_ELIGIBLE) with `dispute_categories`, and Transaction Search carries `protection_eligibility` ([Payments schema](https://developer.paypal.com/api/payments/v2/schema.json)), so Standing can show per order whether PayPal itself marks it covered, which is a concrete reason to add tracking that does not overclaim. The PayPal Agent Toolkit (npm 1.11.0, 5 Nov 2026) and a sandbox MCP endpoint offer an "agentic" story, and actions can be enabled one by one; but its dispute tools include `accept_dispute_claim` and its payments tools create orders and refunds, so it fits only if no write tool is registered ([agent-toolkit repo](https://github.com/paypal/agent-toolkit), [PayPal MCP](https://developer.paypal.com/ai-tools/mcp-server.md)). Ask Standing already answers only from code-rendered facts, so swapping in toolkit reads adds a buzzword more than a capability.

Marketplace health dashboards supply the design vocabulary. eBay shows each metric against an explicit threshold, a look-back window and **the level the seller would get if evaluated today**, and tells sellers which metric to fix first ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-seller-performance/seller-performance-standards?id=4347)); Etsy shows the current window beside the one that explains today's badge and skips metrics with no data ([Etsy](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/29665255990039)). Standing's deterministic rules suit eBay's per-rule threshold table well. Amazon's single 0–1,000 score ([Amazon AHR](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G521)) is the wrong model: PayPal publishes no score, so a banded number would look like a PayPal metric Standing invented.

## AG Grid, PayPal fields and the dispute loop earn the most per day

The hackathon asks that projects "meaningfully use both PayPal and AI" with no fixed tracks ([PayPal blog](https://developer.paypal.com/community/blog/PayPal_AI_Hackathon)), and an aggregator reports judges want PayPal integration "central, not minor" ([aggregator](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/paypal-ai-hackathon-2026)). That favours deepening the PayPal surface over adding AI. For the sponsor prize (reported at $5,000 / $2,000 / 3 × $1,000), Standing currently uses one grid with sort, filter, resize and quick filter. AG Grid Community, free under MIT, also offers pinned rows via `PinnedRowModule`, custom cell renderers, column groups, column pinning and CSV export; row grouping, master/detail, set filter, sparklines, status bar and integrated charts are Enterprise ([licensing](https://www.ag-grid.com/javascript-data-grid/licensing/), [pricing comparison](https://www.ag-grid.com/license-pricing/), [row pinning](https://www.ag-grid.com/react-data-grid/row-pinning/)). An Enterprise trial key is a trap: the repo is public, and judging (1–15 Dec) lands after a trial started now expires. The 7 Nov finding that orders created with an item list return item names through Transaction Search also changes the picture: the catalogue and AUP check can now match real sandbox orders, so the demo orders should be regenerated with item lists rather than labelled as unmatched.

Several market patterns must stay out entirely: automatic refunds at the inquiry stage or Stripe-style auto-submit before a deadline (writes without the seller's send), Chargeflow-style win prediction, a single banded health score, AI judging whether uploaded documents are acceptable, Payouts, and any copy saying Standing releases funds, lowers reserves, prevents holds, qualifies sellers for Seller Protection or is PayPal-approved.

A sensible sequence for the two build days is: on 8 Nov morning, a one-hour dispute spike (create a sandbox dispute as the buyer, call `require-evidence`, check scope and status) decides whether #6 is attempted; the rest of 8 Nov goes to #1–#3, which together give the grid real PayPal columns to display. On 9 Nov, #6 and #7 if the spike passed, otherwise #4 and #5; #8 fits wherever half a day is left. Each item adds a concrete video beat: a pinned row of held money, a column showing PayPal's own protection flag, and a pack that leaves Standing and lands on a PayPal dispute with a debug ID.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Standing's competitive position: its moat is not prediction, which Payout Guard shows any team can copy, but **disciplined honesty around PayPal writes in a market where nearly every rival overclaims**. The highest-scoring additions are the ones that make that discipline visible against PayPal's own data, so the grid shows "what PayPal says" next to "what Standing says", and the cited pack travels into a real sandbox dispute through the same preview and cancel gate as the tracking fix. Read-only facts PayPal already returns (protection eligibility, withheld balance, hold codes, response due dates) score better per day than more AI, because they deepen the PayPal integration judges are told to weigh, and they cost nothing in rule risk.

The larger product lesson, beyond the hackathon, is that prevention alone misses where sellers hurt most. Permanent limitations, document rejections and new-market onboarding failures all happen after or around PayPal's decision, and PayPal is not obliged to explain them. Standing's evidence ledger and refusal rules already suit a "first 24 hours" role, which is a credible next chapter to name in the video as roadmap. Unknowns remain: the official sponsor-prize criteria and the competing gallery could not be read (Devpost blocked automated access), sandbox may never show withheld funds, and no public data shows that tracking, complete submissions or any tool changes PayPal's outcomes, which is exactly why Standing should never say it does.
